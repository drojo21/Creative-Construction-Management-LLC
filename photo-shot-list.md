# Job-Site Photo Shot List
## Creative Construction Management LLC · ROC 355392

A practical guide Jesus (or anyone on the crew) can pull up on their phone at a job site. Follow this and you'll end up with gallery-ready photos, ad creative, and social content from every job — no extra trips, no hired photographer needed.

**Goal:** walk away from every project with 15–25 usable photos spread across three phases (before, during, after). That's enough to populate the gallery, feed Facebook posts for months, and rotate ad creative without running out.

---

## Gear — the minimum you need

You already have it.

- A modern smartphone (iPhone 11 or newer, or any Android flagship from 2020+)
- Clean lens (microfiber cloth — phones live in pockets with dust and drywall)
- 30 seconds per shot

Optional upgrades, in order of ROI:
1. **A $15 phone tripod** with a Bluetooth shutter. Game-changer for wide-angle interior shots. Search "phone tripod bluetooth" on Amazon.
2. **A cheap LED panel light** ($40). For dark interior rooms mid-remodel.
3. **A drone** (only if you want aerial commercial shots — not needed for residential).

---

## The three-phase rule

Every job gets photographed three times. Put it on the calendar.

### Phase 1 · BEFORE (20 min, day 1 on site)

Take these **before any demo starts** — ideally before you even lay down floor protection.

- [ ] **Straight-on hero shot** — the single widest angle of the space as it exists today
- [ ] **Corner view, left** — stand in one corner, shoot diagonally across
- [ ] **Corner view, right** — same, opposite corner
- [ ] **Detail of what's wrong** — the dated cabinets, the cracked tile, the water damage, whatever the customer hired you to fix
- [ ] **Wide shot from the entry** — looking into the room from the doorway at standing height
- [ ] **A "before with the customer standing in it"** — if they're willing. Adds massive human element for social. Get them to sign the release form *before* you take it.

**Quantity target: 6–8 photos.**

### Phase 2 · DURING (5 min at 3 key moments)

These are the money shots for social and ads — they show the work happening.

Shoot at each of these three milestones:

**Milestone A — Demo complete / framing started**
- [ ] Wide shot of the space stripped back to studs / subfloor
- [ ] A "work in progress" action shot — crew member with tools, genuinely working
- [ ] Tight detail of a rough-in moment (new plumbing lines, new electrical, framing for a new wall)

**Milestone B — Halfway point / drywall up**
- [ ] Wide shot of the space with drywall done, before paint
- [ ] A detail shot of something technical the homeowner won't normally see (e.g., LED dimmer wiring, pex manifold, insulation quality)

**Milestone C — Just before finish work starts**
- [ ] Wide shot showing paint done, ready for cabinets/fixtures/tile
- [ ] Close-up of the crew or a tool laid out on the site (no stock "hands shaking" shots — make it real)

**Quantity target: 7–9 photos.**

### Phase 3 · AFTER (30 min, on final walkthrough day)

This is the single most important shoot of the project. Do it before anyone moves furniture back in or clutters the space.

- [ ] **Hero shot — EXACT same angle as the Phase 1 "straight-on hero"** (this is your before/after pair — do not screw this one up)
- [ ] **Hero shot — EXACT same angles as the two Phase 1 corner views** (another before/after pair each)
- [ ] **A wide shot lit naturally** — open the blinds, turn off artificial lights, shoot during the "golden hour" window if possible (90 min before sunset — Tucson light is beautiful then)
- [ ] **Three detail shots** — the best new finishes: a corner of the cabinetry, the tile transition, the grout line where nobody can see a flaw. Rule: if it looks good up close, it's a gallery shot.
- [ ] **One "lived-in" photo** — set a book on the counter, a plant on the vanity, a bowl of fruit. Makes finished spaces feel like homes, not showrooms.
- [ ] **Team photo at the site** — you + crew + happy customer if they agree. Get the release form signed.

**Quantity target: 8–10 photos.**

## Angles — the four-shot formula

Every good interior photo is one of four angles. Memorize these four and you'll never have to think about composition again.

### Angle 1 — The Hero (straight-on, eye-level)

Stand in the doorway or at the natural entry to the room. Hold the phone at chest height. Don't tilt. Shoot horizontally (landscape). This is the "main" photo of the space.

**Use for:** before/after comparisons, website gallery headers, Google Business Profile cover photos.

### Angle 2 — The Corner (diagonal, 45°)

Stand in a corner of the room. Shoot diagonally toward the opposite corner. This makes small rooms look bigger and shows two walls plus ceiling/floor — more information per frame.

**Use for:** kitchens and bathrooms especially. Real estate photographers use this angle for 80% of their shots. For a reason.

### Angle 3 — The Detail (tight, 6–12 inches away)

Close-up of one specific finish: grout line, cabinet pull, tile edge, lighting fixture, faucet. Shoot horizontally. Phone touching or nearly touching the surface.

**Use for:** Instagram carousels, Facebook ad creative that needs to stop the scroll. Detail shots outperform wide shots for paid social.

### Angle 4 — The Context (wide, step back)

Step all the way back to the edge of the space, squat down slightly (phone at hip height), and capture the whole room plus the view beyond into the next room. Gives scale and makes the space feel expansive.

**Use for:** whole-home remodels, "open concept" projects, client case studies.

## Lighting — the three rules

Good lighting hides a lot of mistakes. Bad lighting kills even perfect work.

### Rule 1 — Daylight beats everything

Shoot at **10am–2pm for exterior** shots (direct sun) and **1hr before sunset for interior** shots (warm diffused light through windows). Avoid midday interior — harsh shadows on every wall.

Tucson exception: in summer (May–September), the 10am–2pm sun is too harsh. Back off to 8am–10am or 4pm–6pm.

### Rule 2 — Turn off every artificial light when shooting interiors

Yes, every light. Incandescent, LED, even bathroom vanity lights. Mixed light temperatures look awful in photos (warm yellow from bulbs + cool blue from windows = your tile looks green). Let natural daylight through the windows do the work.

Exception: if a room has NO natural light (basement bath, interior closet), then turn ON all the LED lights and shoot.

### Rule 3 — Clean the space before you shoot

Every tool on the floor. Every drop cloth in the frame. Every paper cup on the counter. It all reads as "unprofessional" in a photo. Two minutes of clean-up before the shutter clicks is the highest-value time you'll spend.

## Crop ratios — what format for what platform

Shoot everything in the phone's default horizontal (landscape) orientation. You can crop to other ratios after, but you can't recover pixels you didn't capture.

| Platform | Target ratio | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Website gallery (the HTML file) | 4:3 horizontal | Matches the CSS grid `aspect-ratio: 4/3` |
| Website hero | 16:9 horizontal | Matches the hero section |
| Google Business Profile cover | 16:9 (1200×628) | Google's spec |
| Facebook feed post | 4:5 vertical OR 1:1 square | Both work; vertical fills more screen |
| Instagram feed | 4:5 vertical or 1:1 square | Same as FB |
| Instagram story / reel | 9:16 vertical | Full screen |
| Facebook ad | 4:5 vertical OR 1:1 square | Avoid 16:9 — gets letterboxed |

**Pro workflow:** shoot landscape on the phone, then in the Photos app duplicate each shot once for each crop needed. Takes 30 seconds.

## File naming — do this every time

When you export photos off the phone (or AirDrop to a laptop), rename them with this pattern:

```
creative-construction-[service]-[city]-[YYYY-MM].jpg
```

Examples:
- `creative-construction-kitchen-remodel-tucson-2026-04.jpg`
- `creative-construction-bathroom-remodel-marana-2026-05.jpg`
- `creative-construction-room-addition-oro-valley-2026-06.jpg`

Google reads filenames for SEO. "IMG_2847.jpg" tells Google nothing. The descriptive filename tells Google a lot.

## Client permission — non-negotiable

**Before you photograph anything inside a customer's home or business — or photograph the customer themselves — get the release form signed.**

See `photo-release-form.md` in this package. Print 10 copies. Keep them in the truck. Hand one to every customer at the initial walkthrough, along with a brief "Hey, we like to document our work for our portfolio — would you mind signing this?" conversation.

**99 out of 100 customers say yes.** The 1 who says no, just skip the photos. Never shoot without the signature.

## Using the photos — where they go

Once you've got the shots off the phone:

### Website gallery (8 placeholders to replace)
Open `index.html`, find `<section id="gallery">`. Each of the 8 `<div class="gallery-item">` blocks has a placeholder like `<span>Kitchen Remodel<br/>&mdash; Tucson 85746</span>`. Replace that `<span>` with `<img src="photos/creative-construction-kitchen-remodel-tucson-2026-04.jpg" alt="Kitchen remodel in Tucson 85746" />`. Upload the `photos/` folder with your deploy.

### Facebook posts (uses photos 2, 4, 5 per the `facebook-posts.md` plan)
Post 2 needs a before/after carousel. Post 4 needs a mid-job framing shot. Post 5 needs a finished-project photo.

### Facebook ads (Campaign 1 wants a hero shot or owner video)
The single strongest performing creative type for contractor ads is a 15-second selfie video of the owner on site. Shoot one with your phone in landscape mode, captioned "Licensed Tucson Contractor · ROC 355392", and you'll outperform stock-photo ads by 2–3x.

### Google Business Profile (needs 10+ photos)
Upload every decent shot. Name them descriptively. Google rewards photo volume — it's one of the strongest local ranking signals.

### Reviews page QR cards
Print cards with a finished-kitchen or finished-bathroom photo on the back, QR code and review link text on the front.

---

## A closing note

You don't need to shoot every single item on these lists for every job. A guest bathroom refresh might have 8 total photos; a 1,200 sq ft addition might have 40. The rule that matters: **before you walk off site on final walkthrough day, the after-photos are done.** Missing the "after" window — once furniture moves back in and someone hangs a towel over the new shower wall — is how contractors end up with zero portfolio after 3 years in business.

One camera roll folder per job. Back it up to Google Drive or iCloud. In six months you'll have a library that lets you write ads, post to Facebook three times a week, and fill any empty slot on the website — for free, forever.
